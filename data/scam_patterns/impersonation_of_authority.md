# Pattern Name: Impersonation of Authority
**Risk Level:** High
**Keywords:** fake rescue logos, cloned organization, official-sounding claims, government affiliation, NGO impersonation

## Description
Impersonation of authority occurs when a fundraiser mimics legitimate organizations or claims affiliations with recognized NGOs, government bodies, or well-known rescue networks. This tactic is used to gain instant credibility and lower donor skepticism without providing verifiable proof.

## Common Indicators
- **Logo or Name Cloning:** Uses logos, fonts, or names closely resembling legitimate rescues (e.g., “SPCA [City Name]” or “PETA Rescue Fund”) without official registration.
- **False Endorsements:** Claims of partnerships, sponsorships, or official approval from recognized authorities or veterinary organizations that cannot be verified.
- **Official Language or Seals:** Documents or posts include certificates, stamps, or badges that appear government-issued but are unverifiable or fabricated.
- **Platform Impersonation:** Fake social media or crowdfunding pages that mirror verified accounts in design and content.

## Why This Matters
Donors often trust organizations with familiar branding or apparent authority. Impersonation manipulates that trust to solicit funds fraudulently. By mimicking legitimate institutions, scammers reduce scrutiny and increase the likelihood of immediate donations.

## Example Scammer Script
> "As endorsed by the National Animal Welfare Society, your donation will save injured puppies today!"  
> *(Investigation reveals the organization does not exist or the account is unaffiliated.)*

## How Legitimate Rescues Differ
Real organizations display verifiable registration numbers, official websites, and legitimate social media handles. They can provide references, documentation, or live contact with staff. Official endorsements are always publicly listed and traceable.
