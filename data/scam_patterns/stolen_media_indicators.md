# Pattern Name: Stolen or Reused Media
**Risk Level:** High
**Keywords:** reused animal photo, reverse image match, stock rescue image, duplicate fundraiser images

## Description
Stolen or reused media refers to images or videos of animals that are taken from unrelated past rescues, stock photography, or previous viral posts and reused to solicit donations. Scammers rely on emotionally powerful visuals while avoiding the effort of documenting a real, current rescue.

## Common Indicators
- **Reverse Image Matches:** The same animal image appears in older or unrelated rescue campaigns, news articles, or stock photo sites.
- **Mismatched Context:** Image metadata, captions, or visual details conflict with the stated location, date, or story of the current fundraiser.
- **Watermarks or Cropping Artifacts:** Visible watermarks, logos, or heavy cropping that suggest the media was taken from another source.
- **Lack of Original Footage:** Only still images are provided, with no live video, timestamped photos, or follow-up media showing ongoing care.

## Why This Matters
Reused media allows scammers to fabricate urgency and credibility without having custody of the animal. It also prevents donors from verifying whether their money supports a real, current rescue or an unrelated past event.

## Example Scammer Script
> "This puppy was found starving on the streets today. Please donate now to save her life."

*(Image later found to originate from a 2019 shelter post in a different country.)*

## How Legitimate Rescues Differ
Legitimate rescues typically share original, time-consistent media, including multiple photos or videos taken during intake, treatment, and recovery. They can often provide live video calls, recent updates, or verifiable links to the original source of the media.
