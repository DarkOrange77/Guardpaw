# Pattern Name: Digital Footprint Anomalies
**Risk Level:** Medium–High
**Keywords:** new account, bot followers, low engagement, no public validation, suspicious activity

## Description
Digital footprint anomalies occur when a fundraiser or rescue account exhibits unusual or suspicious online behavior that undermines credibility. This includes brand-new accounts, inflated follower counts with minimal engagement, or a lack of verifiable online history, which are common in fraudulent campaigns.

## Common Indicators
- **New or Recently Created Accounts:** Social media or domain accounts created shortly before fundraising begins.
- **Bot-Like Metrics:** Large number of followers with few comments, likes, or interactions; uniform or repetitive usernames across multiple accounts.
- **Minimal Online Presence:** Limited posts, no history of rescue activity, or absence of previous campaigns.
- **Lack of Community Validation:** No tagged volunteers, adopters, or publicly verifiable third-party supporters.

## Why This Matters
A weak or anomalous digital footprint reduces the ability to cross-check claims, verify organizational legitimacy, or obtain independent references. Scammers rely on these “clean slates” to avoid scrutiny and appear legitimate.

## Example Scammer Script
> "Follow our new rescue account for updates! All our animals are in urgent need, but we’re just starting online."  
> *(Account created 2 days ago, 50,000 followers, 0 comments.)*

## How Legitimate Rescues Differ
Real rescues have traceable online histories, including past campaigns, public acknowledgments from volunteers or adopters, and sustained engagement with the community. Accounts often show consistent, verifiable activity over months or years.
